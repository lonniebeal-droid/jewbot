## MODULE 0 — REAL TALK FIRST

Before anything else, here's the truth nobody in those Instagram reels tells you.

### 0.1 Most beginners lose money

Studies of day traders keep finding the same thing: **most people who try short-term trading lose money, and only a small slice make steady profits over years.** That's not a reason to never learn. It's a reason to learn slowly, risk small and treat the first months as school, not a payday.

Trading is not a job replacement. Don't quit a shift because of a good week.

### 0.2 The order of money (do these BEFORE trading)

Think of it like a staircase. You don't skip steps.

1. **Bills paid.** Rent, lights, phone, car note, insurance, gas for the car you drive for work. Trading money never comes from bill money.  
2. **Emergency fund.** Gig work has slow weeks, so put away at least **one month of bills** to start, and build toward **three to six months**. If your car breaks down and you have no cushion, you'll be tempted to "trade your way out." That's how accounts get blown up.  
3. **High-interest debt handled.** If a credit card charges you 25% a year, paying it down is a guaranteed 25% "return." No trader beats that reliably.  
4. **Only then: trading money.** This is money that, if it went to $0 tomorrow, would hurt your feelings but not your rent.

### 0.3 "Money you can afford to lose": put a number on it

Write this down: *"My trading account is $____. If I lose all of it, my bills still get paid and my emergency fund is untouched."*

If you can't fill that sentence in honestly, your number is $0 for now. Paper trade only. That's fine; you'll still learn everything in here.

### 0.4 Paper trade first, always

Most big brokers have a free paper-trading mode (Module 5 lists a few). You'll run the **30-day paper plan in Module 6** before you put one real dollar in. If you can't make money with fake money while following your rules, real money won't fix that. Real money makes it harder, because now your nerves are involved.

### 0.5 What trading is and isn't

- Trading **is**: making small, planned bets where you know your exit before you get in, and keeping your losses small so you survive long enough to get good.  
- Trading **is not**: guessing, chasing hype, following a "signal group," or betting rent money on a feeling.  
- **Investing** (buying broad funds and holding for years) is a different thing from **trading** (holding for days or hours). For most people, long-term investing in low-cost broad funds is the calmer path. This course is about trading skills, but learning risk math helps with both.

### 0.6 Taxes, quick

Profits from trades you held a year or less are generally taxed like regular income. Keep every record, and your broker sends you a tax form (a 1099-B). If your income is modest, the IRS's free **VITA** program has volunteer sites around Atlanta that do taxes for free. Ask them about trading gains and losses.

> - Trading money = money you can lose without missing rent.  
> - Paper trade first. Thirty days minimum.
