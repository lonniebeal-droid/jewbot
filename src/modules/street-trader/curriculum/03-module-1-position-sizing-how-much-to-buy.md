## MODULE 1 — POSITION SIZING (HOW MUCH TO BUY)

This is the most important module in the course. Most beginners blow up because they **bought too much**, not because they picked the wrong stock.

### 1.1 The 1% rule

**Never risk more than 1% of your account on one trade.**

"Risk" means the money you lose **if your stop gets hit**, not what you spent. You can spend $250 on a trade and only risk $10, because your stop gets you out before it all goes.

Why 1%? Because you WILL have losing streaks. Look at what ten losses in a row does to a $1,000 account:

| Risk per trade | After 10 straight losses | Total damage |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 1% | $904.38 | down 9.6% |
| 2% | $817.07 | down 18.3% |
| 5% | $598.74 | down 40.1% |
| 10% | $348.68 | down 65.1% |

*(Math: each loss takes that % off what's LEFT. 1%: $1,000 × 0.99¹⁰ = $904.38. 10%: $1,000 × 0.90¹⁰ = $348.68.)*

At 1%, a terrible streak is a bad week. At 10%, it's game over.

### 1.2 The formula

> **Shares = (Account × Risk %) ÷ (Entry − Stop)**

In plain words:

1. **Account × Risk %** = the most dollars you're allowed to lose on this trade (your "risk budget").  
2. **Entry − Stop** = how much you lose **per share** if the stop gets hit.  
3. Divide budget by per-share loss = how many shares you can buy.  
4. **Always round DOWN** to a whole share. Rounding up means you're risking more than your budget.

### 1.3 Worked example: $500 account

- Account: $500. Risk 1%: $500 × 0.01 = **$5.00** risk budget.  
- You plan to buy at **$10.00** and your stop is **$9.50**.  
- Loss per share: $10.00 − $9.50 = **$0.50**.  
- Shares: $5.00 ÷ $0.50 = **10 shares**.  
- Cost of the position: 10 × $10.00 = **$100**.  
- If the stop hits: 10 × $0.50 = **$5.00 lost**, which is exactly 1%. ✅

Notice you spent $100 but only risked $5. That's the whole game.

### 1.4 Worked example: $1,000 account

- Account: $1,000. Risk 1%: **$10.00**.  
- Entry **$25.00**, stop **$24.00**. Loss per share: **$1.00**.  
- Shares: $10.00 ÷ $1.00 = **10 shares**.  
- Cost: 10 × $25.00 = **$250**.  
- If the stop hits: 10 × $1.00 = **$10.00 lost** (1%). ✅

### 1.5 Worked example: $5,000 account

- Account: $5,000. Risk 1%: **$50.00**.  
- Entry **$40.00**, stop **$38.50**. Loss per share: **$1.50**.  
- Shares: $50.00 ÷ $1.50 = 33.33… → round DOWN → 33 shares (raw formula).  
- Cost: 33 × $40.00 = $1,320. That's $1,320 ÷ $5,000 = 26.4% of the account. Hold up: that's over the 25% cap (see 1.6). Cap = $5,000 × 25% = $1,250, and $1,250 ÷ $40.00 = 31.25, so round DOWN to 31 shares.  
- Final: **31 shares** × $40.00 = **$1,240 position** (24.8% of the account). If the stop hits: 31 × $1.50 = **$46.50 lost** (about 0.93%, under your 1%). ✅ When the formula and the cap disagree, the cap wins.

### 1.6 Two traps

**Trap 1: the formula says buy more than you have.** $500 account, entry $5.00, stop $4.95. Loss per share is $0.05, so $5.00 ÷ $0.05 = 100 shares = 100 × $5.00 = **$500**, which is your whole account. A stop that tight can get skipped right past (see Trap 2). **House rule: no single position bigger than 25% of your account while you're learning.** For $500 that's $125 max, so 25 shares at $5.00.

**Trap 2: stops aren't guaranteed.** If a stock closes at $10 and opens the next morning at $8 on bad news (that's called a "gap"), your $9.50 stop sells around $8, not $9.50. Your loss is bigger than planned. That's why we keep risk small, avoid holding through earnings reports while learning, and stay away from wild penny stocks.

### 1.7 Wider stop = fewer shares

Same $1,000 account, same $10 risk budget, entry $25:

- Stop $24.50 → $0.50 per share → 20 shares.  
- Stop $24.00 → $1.00 per share → 10 shares.  
- Stop $23.00 → $2.00 per share → 5 shares.

Your **stop goes where the chart says you're wrong** (Module 3). Then the math tells you the size. **Never move the stop just so you can buy more shares.**

### 1.8 Fractional shares

Some brokers let you buy part of a share. If you're using them, the formula still works; you just don't have to round to a whole number. Still round down a little rather than up.

> **LOCK IT IN — Module 1**  
> 

> - Risk = what you lose if the stop hits. It's not what you spend.  
> - Shares = (Account × 1%) ÷ (Entry − Stop). Round DOWN.  
> - No position over 25% of the account while learning.  
> - The stop comes first, then the size. Never the other way around.
