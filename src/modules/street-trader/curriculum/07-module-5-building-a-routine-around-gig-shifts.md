## MODULE 5 — BUILDING A ROUTINE AROUND GIG SHIFTS

Consistency beats intensity. You don't need to watch the screen all day. You need a routine you can actually keep while working.

### 5.1 Know the clock

- U.S. stock market regular hours: **9:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. Eastern**, Monday through Friday (closed on market holidays). Atlanta is on Eastern time, so there's no conversion to do.  
- The first 30 minutes after the open are the wildest. Beginners should **wait until at least 10:00 a.m.** before entering anything.

### 5.2 Pick a routine that fits your shifts

**Morning driver/runner (working 7 a.m. to 3 p.m.):**

- Night before (20 min): review charts and write your plan (entry, stop, target, shares).  
- Place orders with stops **before** your shift, or just stick to the after-close review.  
- **Do not trade while driving.** Georgia's Hands-Free law bans holding or handling your phone while driving. It's also how wrecks and bad trades both happen.

**Evening/night shift (working 4 p.m. to midnight):**

- Morning (30 min, after 10 a.m.): pre-market check, then place or adjust orders.  
- Midday: one quick check on a break.  
- Before your shift: make sure every position has a stop order in place.

**Weekend-heavy gig worker:**

- Swing trade only (hold a few days). Plan on Sunday, check briefly on weekday breaks.

### 5.3 The 15-minute pre-market check

1. **Calendar check (2 min):** Any big news today for your stocks, like earnings reports? While you're learning, **don't hold a stock through its earnings report.**  
2. **Big-picture check (3 min):** Is the overall market up or down this week? Pull up a broad market fund chart on the daily view. If everything's falling, trade smaller or sit out.  
3. **Watchlist (5 min):** 3 to 5 stocks max that are in uptrends near support. Quality over quantity.  
4. **Write the plan (5 min):** for each possible trade, write entry, stop, target, risk/reward and shares (Module 1 formula). If it isn't 2:1, delete it.

Then close the app. No plan, no trade.

### 5.4 The trade journal (use a free Google Sheet)

Make a sheet with these columns:

| Column | What goes in it |
| :---- | :---- |
| Date | Trade date |
| Ticker | Stock symbol |
| Account size | Account value before the trade |
| Risk % | Usually 1% |
| Entry | Planned buy price |
| Stop | Planned stop price |
| Target | Planned target |
| Shares | From the formula |
| R:R | (Target − Entry) ÷ (Entry − Stop) |
| Exit price | Where you actually got out |
| P/L $ | (Exit − Entry) × Shares |
| Followed plan? | Yes / No |
| Why I took it | One sentence |
| Feeling | Calm / FOMO / revenge / bored |
| Lesson | One sentence |

**Copy-paste formulas** (if Account size is in column C, Risk % in D, Entry in E, Stop in F, Target in G, Exit in J):

- Shares (H): `=ROUNDDOWN((C2*D2)/(E2-F2),0)`  
- R:R (I): `=(G2-E2)/(E2-F2)`  
- P/L $ (K): `=(J2-E2)*H2`  
- Expectancy (bottom of sheet, P/L in K2:K100): `=AVERAGE(K2:K100)`, your average dollars per trade.

### 5.5 The weekly review (Sunday, 30 minutes)

1. Count trades, wins and losses. Calculate win %.  
2. Average win, average loss → **expectancy** (Module 2).  
3. **How many times did you break a rule?** This is the most important number. Aim for zero.  
4. Look at your "Feeling" column. Did your losses come on FOMO or revenge days?  
5. Write one thing to keep doing and one thing to fix.  
6. Did you hit the daily or weekly stop? If so, what led up to it?

### 5.6 Free tools only

- **Broker paper trading.** Several big U.S. brokers offer free paper/simulated trading inside their apps (for example Webull's paper trading and Charles Schwab's thinkorswim paperMoney). Check the current terms in the app; features change.  
- **TradingView free tier.** Charts, drawing support and resistance lines, moving averages and volume, all free. It also has a paper-trading feature.  
- **Google Sheets** for your journal (free with a Google account).  
- **Investor.gov and FINRA** for free education and checks.  
- **Free tax help:** the IRS VITA program at Atlanta-area volunteer sites.

**You don't need to pay for courses, signals, bots, or "indicators."** If a tool costs money, the free-first rule applies: prove you're profitable on paper with free tools first.

> **LOCK IT IN — Module 5**  
> 

> - Market hours are 9:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. ET. Beginners wait until 10:00.  
> - Plan before or after shifts. Never trade while driving.  
> - 15-minute pre-market check. No plan, no trade.  
> - Journal every trade. Review every Sunday.  
> - Free tools only until you've proven yourself.
