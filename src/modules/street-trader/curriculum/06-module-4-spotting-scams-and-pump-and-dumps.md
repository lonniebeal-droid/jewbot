## MODULE 4 — SPOTTING SCAMS AND PUMP-AND-DUMPS

More beginners lose money to scams than to bad trades. Scammers go after people who work hard and want a way up, which is exactly who this course is for. Learn these patterns cold.

### 4.1 "Signal" groups (Telegram, Discord, Instagram, TikTok)

**The pitch:** "Join my VIP group, I post the trades, you just copy. $99/month." Or a free group that "upgrades" you later.

**What's really happening:**

- Many of these people make their money **from your subscription fee**, not from trading.  
- Some post a stock, then **sell their own shares to the members who rush in to buy**. You're their exit.  
- Wins get screenshotted; losses get deleted.

**Rule:** If someone's selling signals, ask why they'd sell a money machine for $99 instead of just running it.

### 4.2 Guaranteed returns

**No real investment guarantees returns.** Anyone who says "guaranteed," "risk-free," or "can't lose" is either lying or doesn't know what they're doing.

**The math check:** "10% a week, guaranteed\!" Compounded over one year: 1.10⁵² ≈ **142×**. So $1,000 would turn into about **$142,000** in a year. If that were real, they'd own the city, not be in your DMs.

### 4.3 Fake screenshots and flexing

Screenshots of huge gains, rented cars, cash stacks, "my students' results." All of it is easy to fake: edited images, demo accounts, cherry-picked days. **Screenshots prove nothing.** Only audited, long-term records mean anything, and scammers never have them.

### 4.4 Penny stock and small-company pumps (pump-and-dump)

**How it works:**

1. Promoters quietly buy a cheap, thinly traded stock at, say, **$0.10**.  
2. They **pump** it: hype posts, fake "news," "this is going to $5\!", group chats, paid promo emails.  
3. Regular folks pile in, and price runs to **$0.50**.  
4. Promoters **dump** (sell everything) to those buyers.  
5. Price crashes to around **$0.08**.

**What it does to you:** You buy $200 worth at $0.50 = **400 shares**. After the crash: 400 × $0.08 = **$32**. You lost **$168, which is 84%**.

**Red flags:** you never heard of the company; there's no real product or sales; the stock trades on the "over-the-counter" markets instead of a major exchange; volume suddenly explodes; there's a rush ("before it's too late\!"); hype is all over social media; a stranger is texting you stock tips.

**Check it free:** look up the company's filings on the SEC's EDGAR database (sec.gov/edgar). No real filings or business? Walk away.

### 4.5 Crypto rug pulls

**How it works:** Somebody launches a new coin or token, hypes it hard, and gets people to buy in. Then the creators **pull the money out** ("pull the rug"), and the coin goes to near zero overnight.

**Red flags:** brand-new coin; anonymous team; "locked" money you can't verify; influencers paid to promote; you can buy but somehow can't sell; promises of huge returns for "early" buyers.

### 4.6 "Account manager," romance and "pig-butchering" scams

**How it works:**

- A friendly stranger messages you by "wrong number" or through a dating app or social media. They're warm, they're patient, and they build trust for weeks.  
- Eventually they mention how they (or their "uncle," or their "mentor") make money on a special trading platform. They help you sign up, and you're assigned an **"account manager."**  
- The app shows your money **growing**, and they might even let you withdraw a little at first to hook you.  
- You put in more. When you try to take out a big amount, suddenly there are **"taxes," "fees," or "verification deposits"** to pay first. The money's gone. The website was fake the whole time.

**Red flags:** an online-only relationship that turns to money; pressure to use **their** app or website; being told to pay with crypto, gift cards, wire transfers or a **crypto ATM/kiosk**; fees required to withdraw your own money.

**Georgia's Attorney General says it plainly: any investment that asks you to use a crypto kiosk is a scam.**

### 4.7 Recovery scams (they come back for round two)

After you get scammed, someone contacts you claiming they can **recover your lost money** "for a fee." They might say they're a lawyer, a "blockchain investigator," or even a government agency. **This is usually the same scammers, or their partners, coming back.** Real government agencies don't charge you to get money back.

### 4.8 The red-flag checklist

Before you put a dollar anywhere, run this list. **One "yes" = stop and verify. Two or more = walk away.**

- [ ] Promises guaranteed, "risk-free," or super-high returns  
- [ ] Pressure to act NOW ("only today," "spots filling up")  
- [ ] Contact came from a stranger: DM, text, "wrong number," dating app  
- [ ] Wants payment by crypto, gift cards, wire, Zelle/Cash App to a person, or a crypto ATM  
- [ ] Wants you to use THEIR app or website, not a well-known broker  
- [ ] Asks you to pay fees or "taxes" before you can withdraw  
- [ ] Shows screenshots instead of verifiable records  
- [ ] Person or firm isn't registered (check below)  
- [ ] Cheap no-name stock with sudden hype and huge volume  
- [ ] "Everybody in our community/church/group is doing it"  
- [ ] Tells you to keep it secret or not to talk to family or your bank  
- [ ] Offers to recover money you lost, for a fee

### 4.9 Where to check and report (all free)

**Check before you trust:**

- **FINRA BrokerCheck**: brokercheck.finra.org. Is this person or firm actually registered to sell investments?  
- **SEC Investor.gov**: investor.gov. Free investor education, plus a search for registered advisers.  
- **SEC EDGAR**: sec.gov/edgar. A real company's actual filings.  
- **FINRA Scam Meter**: tools.finra.org/scam_meter. Answer a few questions and get red flags back.  
- **Georgia Secretary of State, Securities Division**: sos.ga.gov. Check whether someone is licensed to sell securities in Georgia. Phone (470) 312-2640.

**Report it:**

- **FTC:** reportfraud.ftc.gov  
- **SEC tips and complaints:** sec.gov/tcr  
- **Georgia Secretary of State Securities Division complaint form:** sos.ga.gov/form/complaint-office-secretary-state-securities-division  
- **FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center:** ic3.gov (online and crypto scams)  
- **CFTC** (crypto/forex/commodities fraud): cftc.gov/complaint  
- **Georgia Attorney General Consumer Protection:** consumer.georgia.gov

**If you already got hit:** stop sending money, call your bank or card company right away, save every message and screenshot, report it to the places above, and **ignore anyone offering to "recover" it for a fee.** There's no shame in it. These are professional criminals, and reporting helps the next person.
