## MODULE 6 — PRACTICE DRILLS, QUIZ AND THE 30-DAY PAPER PLAN

### 6.1 Drills (do them on paper first, then check the answer key)

**Drill 1: Position size.** Account $800, risk 1%. Entry $30.00, stop $29.20. How many shares? How much does the position cost? What do you lose if the stop hits?

**Drill 2: Position size with rounding.** Account $2,000, risk 1%. Entry $15.00, stop $14.20. How many shares?

**Drill 3: The 25% cap.** Account $1,000, risk 2% (an experienced-trader setting, just for practice). Entry $12.00, stop $11.40. How many shares does the formula give? What's the position cost? Does it pass the 25%-of-account cap? If not, what's the max shares under the cap?

**Drill 4: Risk/reward.** Entry $50, stop $48, target $55. What's the ratio? Take it or skip it?

**Drill 5: Expectancy.** You win 60% of trades, average win $15, average loss $20. What's your expectancy per trade? Over 40 trades?

**Drill 6: Drawdown.** Your account drops 40%. What % gain gets you back to even?

**Drill 7: Half-percent risk.** Account $5,000, risk 0.5%. Entry $50, stop $48. Shares?

**Drill 8: Chart read.** A candle opens $20.00, closes $19.40, high $20.10, low $18.50. Green or red? Body size? Which wick is long, and what might it mean?

**Drill 9: Scam spotting.** A "wrong number" text turns into a friendly chat. Two weeks later they show you a trading app where their account is up 300%, and they offer to have their "account manager" help you. You deposit $200, and the app shows $260 a week later. When you try to withdraw, they say pay a 15% "tax" first. Name every red flag.

**Drill 10: Daily stop.** Account $1,500. Daily stop is 3%. You're down $38 after two trades. Can you take a third trade that risks 1%?

### 6.2 Quiz (true or false)

1. If I risk 1% per trade, I can only spend 1% of my account on a trade.  
2. A 70% win rate always means the trader is profitable.  
3. It's OK to move my stop lower if the trade is going against me, as long as I believe in it.  
4. Losing 50% requires a 50% gain to get back to even.  
5. A real investment can't guarantee returns.  
6. If a "recovery" company asks for a fee to get my scammed money back, that's a red flag.  
7. Beginners should wait until at least 10:00 a.m. ET before entering trades.  
8. Screenshots of big gains are good proof that a signal group works.

### 6.3 ANSWER KEY

**Drill 1:** Risk = $800 × 0.01 = **$8.00**. Per share = $30.00 − $29.20 = **$0.80**. Shares (raw formula) = $8.00 ÷ $0.80 = **10 shares**. Cost = 10 × $30 = **$300**. Loss if stopped = 10 × $0.80 = **$8.00**. That's $300 ÷ $800 = 37.5% of the account. Hold up: that's over the 25% cap (see 1.6). Cap = $800 × 25% = $200, and $200 ÷ $30.00 = 6.67, so round DOWN to **6 shares**. Final: 6 shares × $30.00 = **$180 position** (22.5% of the account). If the stop hits: 6 × $0.80 = **$4.80 lost** (0.6%, under your 1%). ✅ When the formula and the cap disagree, the cap wins.

**Drill 2:** Risk = $2,000 × 0.01 = **$20.00**. Per share = $15.00 − $14.20 = **$0.80**. Shares = $20 ÷ $0.80 = **25 shares**. (Cost 25 × $15 = $375; loss if stopped 25 × $0.80 = $20.)

**Drill 3:** Risk = $1,000 × 0.02 = **$20.00**. Per share = $12.00 − $11.40 = **$0.60**. Shares = $20 ÷ $0.60 = 33.33 → **33 shares**. Cost = 33 × $12 = **$396**, which is 39.6% of the account. **Fails** the 25% cap ($250). Max under the cap = $250 ÷ $12 = 20.8 → **20 shares** ($240 cost; risk 20 × $0.60 = $12, which is 1.2%).

**Drill 4:** Risk $2, reward $5 → **2.5:1.** Passes the 2:1 rule, so it's OK to consider (if the chart and the rest of your plan agree).

**Drill 5:** (0.60 × $15) − (0.40 × $20) = $9 − $8 = **\+$1.00 per trade**. Over 40 trades: **\+$40**. It's positive, but barely. Work on bigger wins or smaller losses.

**Drill 6:** 0.40 ÷ (1 − 0.40) = 0.40 ÷ 0.60 = **66.7%**.

**Drill 7:** Risk = $5,000 × 0.005 = **$25.00**. Per share = **$2.00**. Shares = 12.5 → **12 shares** (cost $600; loss if stopped $24).

**Drill 8:** **Red** (closed below the open). Body = $20.00 − $19.40 = **$0.60**. Top wick = $20.10 − $20.00 = $0.10. Bottom wick = $19.40 − $18.50 = **$0.90, the long one.** Sellers drove it down to $18.50, but buyers pushed it back up $0.90 before the close. That could be buyers defending that area, so watch the next candles before deciding anything.

**Drill 9:** Stranger/"wrong number" contact; online relationship turning to money; their app, not a known broker; "account manager"; unrealistic gains (300%); a fee or "tax" to withdraw. **It's a pig-butchering scam.** Stop depositing, don't pay the "tax," save the messages, report to the FTC, IC3 and the GA SOS, and call your bank.

**Drill 10:** Daily stop = $1,500 × 0.03 = **$45**. You're down $38, so you have **$7** left before the cutoff. A 1% trade risks **$15**. If it lost, you'd be down $53, past your $45 stop. **No third trade today.** (You also lost two in a row; a third loss would hit the 3-loss stop.) Walk away.

**Quiz:** 1 **False**: 1% is what you RISK (loss at the stop), not what you spend. 2 **False**: see Trader A, who wins 70% and loses $2 per trade. 3 **False**: never move a stop down. 4 **False**: you need **100%**. 5 **True.** 6 **True.** 7 **True.** 8 **False**: screenshots are easy to fake.

### 6.4 The 30-day paper-trading plan

**Setup (Day 0):**

- Open a paper-trading account with a broker's simulator or TradingView. Set the fake balance to the **real** amount you'd actually trade ($500, $1,000, whatever). Practicing with $100,000 fake dollars teaches bad habits.  
- Build the Google Sheet journal from Module 5.  
- Write your rules on one page: 1% risk, 2:1 minimum, 25% position cap, daily stop 3%/3 losses, weekly stop 6%, no trading before 10:00 a.m., no trading while driving, no holding through earnings.

**Week 1 (Days 1–7): Learn the charts, no trades.**

- Daily: 20 minutes on TradingView. Mark support and resistance on 5 stocks. Label each one uptrend, downtrend or sideways.  
- Do all 10 drills in Module 6 until you get them right without looking.  
- Goal: you can explain any candle and find a floor and ceiling in under 2 minutes.

**Week 2 (Days 8–14): Plan trades, place few.**

- Daily: the 15-minute pre-market check. Write full plans (entry, stop, target, shares, R:R) for 1–2 setups.  
- Place **no more than 1 paper trade per day.** Every one gets a stop order the moment you enter.  
- Journal every trade, including how you felt.

**Week 3 (Days 15–21): Follow the rules perfectly.**

- Same routine, up to 2 paper trades per day.  
- Goal: **zero rule breaks** this week. A losing trade that followed every rule counts as a success; a winning trade that broke a rule counts as a failure.  
- Sunday review: win %, average win, average loss, expectancy, rule breaks.

**Week 4 (Days 22–30): Prove it.**

- Keep going at 1–2 trades a day.  
- Day 30: run the full numbers on all your trades since Week 2.

**The Day-30 scorecard (be honest):**

| Check | Pass if |
| :---- | :---- |
| Trades logged | At least 20 |
| Rule breaks | 0 in the last 2 weeks |
| Expectancy | Positive (above $0 per trade) |
| Biggest single loss | No more than about 1% of the account (gaps aside) |
| Daily stop hit | You stopped every time it hit |
| Emergency fund and bills | Still fully covered (Module 0) |

**Pass all six?** You can consider going live with real money that you can afford to lose, **at half size** (0.5% risk per trade) for the next 30 days. Real money feels different. **Miss any?** Run another 30-day paper round. There's no rush. The market will be there next month, and your money will still be in your pocket.
