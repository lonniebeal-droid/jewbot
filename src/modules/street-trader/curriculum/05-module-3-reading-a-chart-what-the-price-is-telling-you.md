## MODULE 3 — READING A CHART (WHAT THE PRICE IS TELLING YOU)

A chart is just a record of what people paid over time. You're not predicting the future. You're reading the story so far and making a small, planned bet.

### 3.1 Candles

Most charts use **candles**. Each candle covers one period of time, like one day or five minutes, and shows four prices:

- **Open:** the first price of that period.  
- **Close:** the last price.  
- **High:** the top price reached.  
- **Low:** the bottom price reached.

The fat part is the **body** (from open to close). The thin lines sticking out are **wicks**, and they show how far price went before getting pushed back.

- **Green (or hollow) candle:** closed higher than it opened. Buyers won that round.  
- **Red (or filled) candle:** closed lower than it opened. Sellers won that round.

**Read one:** Open $10.00, close $10.50, high $10.80, low $9.90.

- Green, because it closed above the open.  
- Body = $10.50 − $10.00 = **$0.50**.  
- Top wick = $10.80 − $10.50 = **$0.30**. Buyers pushed to $10.80 but got pushed back a bit.  
- Bottom wick = $10.00 − $9.90 = **$0.10**. It dipped a little early and then recovered.

**Patterns worth knowing, in plain words:**

- **Long bottom wick:** sellers pushed it down hard, but buyers stepped in and pushed it back up. It can mean buyers are defending that price.  
- **Long top wick:** buyers tried to run it up, and sellers slapped it back down. It can mean selling pressure up there.  
- **Tiny body, wicks both ways:** a tie, with nobody in control. Wait for more info.

One candle is a clue, not a verdict. Look at what's around it.

### 3.2 Support and resistance (the floor and the ceiling)

- **Support (the floor):** a price where the stock keeps bouncing **up** from. Buyers keep showing up there.  
- **Resistance (the ceiling):** a price where the stock keeps getting **turned down**. Sellers keep showing up there.

**How to find them:** zoom out and look for spots where price turned around **two or more times**. Draw a horizontal line there. Think of them as zones, not exact pennies.

**How you use them:**

- Buy near support, with your **stop just below support**. If the floor breaks, your idea was wrong, so you're out.  
- Your **target goes near the next resistance** above.  
- Then check the math: is the distance to the target at least 2× the distance to the stop? If not, skip the trade.  
- When a ceiling breaks and price stays above it, the old ceiling often becomes the new floor.

### 3.3 Trend (which way the street is running)

- **Uptrend:** each high is higher than the last and each low is higher than the last. The stairs go up.  
- **Downtrend:** lower highs and lower lows. The stairs go down.  
- **Sideways (range):** bouncing between a floor and a ceiling.

**Beginner rule: only buy in uptrends.** Don't try to catch the bottom of a stock that's falling. A falling stock can keep falling longer than your account can last.

**Moving average, simply:** a line on the chart showing the **average closing price over the last X days**. A 50-day moving average is the average of the last 50 closes. If price is above a rising 50-day line, the trend is generally up. It's a free setting on TradingView.

### 3.4 Volume (how many people showed up)

Volume bars usually sit at the bottom of the chart. They show how many shares traded.

- **A big move on big volume** means lots of people agree, so it's more believable.  
- **A big move on tiny volume** means few people are involved, so it's easier to fake and easier to reverse.  
- **A sudden volume explosion in a cheap stock you never heard of, with no real news?** That's a red flag for a pump (Module 4).

### 3.5 Timeframes (zoom level)

The same stock looks different on different zoom levels:

- **Daily chart:** each candle is one day. Use it for the big picture and trend.  
- **Hourly / 15-minute:** each candle is an hour or 15 minutes. Use it to fine-tune your entry.  
- **1-minute:** fast and noisy. Beginners should skip it.

**Rule: check the daily chart first.** If the daily trend is down, a pretty 5-minute chart doesn't change that. For gig workers, **daily charts plus trades held a few days** fit your schedule best, because you can do the work before or after shifts instead of staring at a phone while driving.

### 3.6 Putting it together: a sample read

"Stock XYZ, daily chart. It's making higher highs and higher lows, and it's above its rising 50-day average, so it's an **uptrend.** It's pulled back to **$48**, where it bounced twice last month (**support**). Today's candle has a long bottom wick at $48 on above-average **volume**, so buyers defended the floor. The ceiling from last month's high is **$54**."

- Entry $50, stop $47.50 (under support), target $54.  
- Risk per share $2.50, reward per share $4.00 → **1.6:1.** That's below 2:1, so **SKIP.**  
- If the entry were $49 instead: risk $1.50, reward $5.00 → **3.3:1.** That passes, so size it with the Module 1 formula.

This is the discipline: **you can like a chart and still pass because the math doesn't work.**

> - Floor (support) is where buyers show up, ceiling (resistance) is where sellers show up.  
> - Beginners buy uptrends only.  
> - Big move \+ big volume = more believable. Huge volume in a no-name cheap stock = careful.
